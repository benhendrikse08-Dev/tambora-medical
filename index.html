See how it works ↓

On a South African highway, the minutes after a crash decide everything, and too often help arrives late or without knowing what it's driving into. Our medical mission is to become a fully autonomous assistant to emergency medical services: first to every scene, and working alongside paramedics from the first second.

It starts on the highway. When our drones spot a crash and someone is in danger, the nearest emergency response teams get an alert, the video and a live feed, so they know what they're facing before they leave. We're also in conversation with insurers who want clear eyes on the road for their claims, and to support this medical mission.

The drone

Built to get there first.

Every Tambora drone is designed and built by us in South Africa, and flown by TamboraAI, the brain that lets it fly itself and understand what's happening on the ground. It doesn't wait to be called out. It's already in the air.

Always on

Patrols 24/7

Flies its stretch of highway day and night. When its battery runs low, it lands on a rooftop dock that swaps the battery automatically and sends it straight back up.

Sees

Eyes that never blink

Reads the road below in real time, lane by lane. It knows what normal traffic looks like, so a crash stands out the instant it happens. Vehicles and people not involved are blurred.

Thinks

TamboraAI on board

Recognises the crash, reads the number plates involved and packages the video into a sealed report, with no one needing to watch a screen.

Reports

Live to the people who need it

Streams a live feed to emergency response teams and our control room, so responders see the scene before they arrive and know exactly what to bring.

Supervised

Safe by design

Flies itself, overseen by licensed remote pilots who can step in at any moment. Every flight follows South African aviation rules.

Learns

Smarter every flight

What one drone learns today, every drone knows tomorrow. Each patrol teaches TamboraAI more about South African roads.

Where we start

Getting help there, fast.

We start by making sure no crash or medical emergency on the road goes unseen. Our first round of drones deploys across Gauteng and the Western Cape in October 2027, patrolling the highway day and night, recognising an accident the moment it happens and sending the alert before anyone has picked up a phone.

Emergency responders see the scene live before they leave, so they know how many people are hurt, how badly, and what they'll need when they arrive. The right ambulances go out the first time, and no minute is lost to guesswork. Every flight also teaches TamboraAI more about our roads, which is the foundation for everything that comes next.

Where we're going

A future where no one waits for help.

Today, when a crash leaves someone trapped on a highway, help is measured in tens of minutes. We're building towards a South Africa where it's measured in seconds.

First, emergency kits stationed along every major highway, carrying defibrillators, trauma supplies and first aid, flown to the scene the moment a crash is seen, so the person beside the patient can start saving a life before the ambulance turns the corner.

Then, autonomous ambulances: aircraft that lift care, and one day patients, over gridlocked traffic and to the places roads don't reach, from the N1 at rush hour to a village two hours from the nearest hospital.

Every drone we fly today brings that future closer. Our goal is simple: no one in South Africa should die waiting for help to arrive.
